# CopyHuman

## The one fact this skill is built on

Style edits do not work.

The StoryScope researchers took AI-generated stories and ran them through a professional-grade rewriting system that removes cliché, purple prose, and redundant exposition. Detection fell from 95.5 to 93.9. A drop of 1.6 points. Almost nothing.

Then they removed every stylistic feature from their model. No word choice, no sentence rhythm, no figurative density. Structure only. It still scored 93.2.

**The fingerprint is not in the words. It is in the shape.** So this skill operates on the shape, and it operates before the draft exists wherever possible.

Never satisfy a CopyHuman request by rewording. Rewording is the failure mode this skill exists to prevent.

## The second fact, which is easy to miss

Do not invert the AI pattern.

Human writers still resolve stories through protagonist choice 46% of the time. Still avoid subplots 57% of the time. Still write linearly most of the time. Human writing scores as human because it is **dispersed** across the possible choices, not because it sits at the opposite extreme.

A story that flips all thirty features is a new formula, and a worse one. It reads as deliberately difficult.

**Target the human rate, not the opposite of the AI rate.** Every number in `references/core-features.md` gives both. Use them.

## The third fact: Emotion is friction, not adjectives

When asked to make writing "human" or "emotional", LLMs reflexively inject synthetic melodrama, poetic sentiment, and emotional adjectives (*"secangkir kehangatan"*, *"keringat cinta petani"*, *"terpukul dan hancur"*). That is catastrophic AI slop.

Real human emotion is evoked by **concrete circumstances, real dilemmas, and physical stakes**—never by spoon-feeding emotional adjectives:
- **In educational/technical courses:** Present the operational dilemma (e.g., harvesting early vs rain rotting cherries) or functional analogies (e.g., green beans with uneven moisture roasting like wet potatoes), not romanticized praise.
- **In founder/business copy:** State the numbers and deadlines (*"kas sisa Rp14 juta, payroll jatuh tempo Jumat"*) instead of manufactured melodrama (*"saya sangat terpukul dan hancur"*).
- **In marketing/product copy:** Name the daily friction (*"buka 5 spreadsheet tiap cek stok"*) instead of abstract pain (*"mengakhiri kepenatan dan frustrasi Anda"*).
- **In narrative/essays:** State feelings plainly (*"dia takut"*) or let physical actions carry it, avoiding the repetitive somatic triad (tight chest, lump in throat, held breath).

## Three modes

Work out which one applies, then say which one you are running.

- **Mode A, Structure First.** Long-form narrative (fiction, novels, screenplays, essays). Run the 10-decision Structure Sheet before drafting.
- **Mode B, Narrative Audit.** A long-form draft already exists. Score it against the 30 StoryScope features, report it, then restructure what needs it.
- **Mode C, Short-Form & Promotional Copy.** Social media captions (Instagram, LinkedIn, X), ads, founder posts, and short promotional narratives. Executes the 6-pillar micro-copy engine directly without forcing a 10-decision narrative sheet.

If the user hands you a draft and asks for something new in the same style, run B on the draft first, then A (or C) on the new piece.

## Mode A: Structure First

Do not write prose yet.

Produce a **Structure Sheet** of ten decisions. For each one, state the choice and the reason. One line each. Then get the user's sign-off, or if they told you to just go, write the sheet and the draft together with the sheet on top.

The ten decisions, with the human rate and the AI rate for calibration:

| # | Decision | AI default | Human rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Timeline.** Straight through, or jumps? | mostly linear (2.12 on 1 to 5) | 2.40, more flashback and flash-forward |
| 2 | **Subplots.** None, one parallel, one contrasting, one unrelated? | no subplots 79% | no subplots 57%, thematically parallel 42% |
| 3 | **What causes the ending.** Protagonist's choice, mixed, or outside force? | protagonist choice 69% | protagonist choice 46% |
| 4 | **How it ends.** Internal acceptance, external action, or unresolved? | internal understanding 47% | 27% |
| 5 | **The moral.** Does the narrator ever say what the story means? | says it 77% | says it 52% |
| 6 | **How emotion arrives.** Body sensation, plain word, or behaviour? | embodied 81%, plain word 8% | embodied 38%, plain word 29% |
| 7 | **How the lead enters.** Described, mid-action, in dialogue, or in thought? | described from outside 52% | 30% |
| 8 | **References.** A real named thing, or a vague echo? | vague echo 72%, named 24% | vague echo 50%, named 47% |
| 9 | **Reader address.** Does the piece ever acknowledge someone is reading? | almost never, 0.07 | 0.28 |
| 10 | **Is the lead morally clean?** | clean 62% roughly, ambivalent 38% | ambivalent 59% |

### The budget rule

Do not deviate on all ten. Pick **three or four** to break hard, and let the rest sit wherever the story naturally wants them. A story that goes nonlinear AND drops the moral AND ends on outside force AND names three real books AND breaks the fourth wall is not human. It is a student film.

Choose the deviations that serve the story. Say why in one clause. If you cannot say why, do not deviate there.

### The default is the enemy, not the choice

The reason AI writing clusters is that it takes the default on every axis without noticing there was an axis. A human writer who thinks about it and still writes linear, protagonist-driven, and moral-free is fine. The sin is not the linear timeline. The sin is not knowing you picked it.

So when you fill in the sheet, write the reason even for the choices where you stay with the common option.

### Then draft

Write the piece from the sheet. While drafting, master these universal hygiene rules:

- **The contrast cadence & negation tail ban:** STRICTLY BAN the formulaic "Bukan sekadar X, melainkan Y" and "Bukan X tapi Y" cadence. In addition, BAN appending negative trailing clauses at the end of sentences (e.g., *", bukan [kata benda]"* or *", bukan kebetulan teknik"*). State what is true directly and declaratively without leaning on what is not.
- **The narrative colon ban (`:`):** BAN colons as narrative connectors mid-story (e.g., "Aturannya tanpa kompromi:"). Do not substitute em-dashes with colons. Split thoughts into separate, clean sentences with periods (`.`).
- **The em-dash tell (`—`):** STRICTLY BANNED in all Indonesian and localized digital copy. The em-dash is a pervasive AI artifact stemming from American editorial training data. Real humans on keyboards and mobile devices reach for commas, parentheses, or clean sentence breaks (`.`). Seeing `—` is an instant dead giveaway.
- **No decorative bolding:** BAN bolding keywords mid-sentence (`**frasa penting**`). Let the prose carry the weight.
- **The somatic proportion rule (Target ~38%, not 0%):** Do not ban physical sensation entirely—humans use it in 38% of stories. The principle is *earned, not reflexive*. Reserve bodily sensations for genuine moments of peak crisis or physical exertion. AI defaults to a repetitive triad: tightening chest, cold sweat, and held breath. Break this: sometimes write plainly that she was scared (humans say the feeling aloud 29% vs AI 8%), and when using physical sensation, tie it to concrete environmental reality (e.g., calloused fingers cramping over a pencil, dry eyes from screen glare).
- **The closing line that explains:** Cut it. If the last paragraph tells the reader what the story was about or delivers a moral lesson, delete the paragraph. Check the second-to-last one too.
- **Sensory tick (smell):** AI reaches for smell in 82% of stories, humans in 57%. Not a strict rule, just a tic worth catching.

## Mode B: Audit

### Step 1, score

Read `references/core-features.md`. Go through all thirty features. For each, mark where the draft actually sits and whether that leans AI or human. Do not guess from vibes. Point to the line or scene that proves it.

### Step 2, report

Give the user a table with three columns: feature, where the draft sits, which way it leans. Then a plain count: how many of the thirty lean AI.

Be honest about the count. If it is a human-written draft that scores clean, say so and stop. Do not invent problems to look useful.

### Step 3, sort the fixes

Split what you found into two lists.

**Structural.** Ending cause, moral statement, timeline, subplots, resolution mode, character entry, reference specificity. These need real rewriting, sometimes a new scene or a cut scene. These are the ones that move the needle.

**Cosmetic.** Word choice, sensory density, prose rhythm. Mention them if they are bad, but tell the user plainly that fixing these will not change how the piece reads structurally. That is the LAMP result: professional-grade prose editing moved detection by 1.6 points.

### Step 4, fix

Rewrite only the structural items, and only the ones the user picks. Show the change as a decision, not a diff: "the ending currently resolves because she decides to forgive him. Moving it so the phone call comes anyway, whether she decided or not."

Never present a reworded sentence as a fix for a structural problem.

## Mode C: Short-Form & Promotional Copy (Captions, Ads, Founder Posts)

Use this mode whenever writing social media captions (Instagram, LinkedIn, X), short ad copy, emails, and promotional narratives. 

Do NOT force the 10-decision narrative sheet here (that is for long-form narrative). Instead, enforce this unified 6-pillar micro-copy engine:

### 1. The Hook: Grounded Reality & Named Specificity
- BAN rhetorical opening questions ("Pernahkah Anda...?", "Mau belajar X tapi bingung...?").
- BAN manufactured hyperbole: Do not invent melodramatic panics over trivial variables (e.g., panicking because water temperature dropped 2 degrees). Ground the opening friction in real, everyday human experience.
- Name real entities: BAN vague placeholders ("sebuah platform", "alat canggih", "suatu masalah"). Name actual tools, platforms, real metrics, prices, and concrete environments (e.g., *grinder, rasio 1:15, ketel, Rp150 ribu*).

### 2. Anti-Over-Specification (Conversational Shorthand)
- When context is clear, do NOT use catalog-level compound nouns. 
- Use natural conversational shorthand (e.g., write "ketel" instead of "ketel leher angsa"; "kacamata" instead of "kacamata pelindung radiasi"). Real practitioners on social media do not talk like equipment manuals.

### 3. Human Observational Warmth & Somatic Baseline
- Measure human comfort and skill through observational warmth ("gerakannya udah mulai santai", "udah biasa nyeduh"), never mechanical evaluative grading ("gerakannya sudah rapi", "kinerjanya optimal").
- Keep physical sensations grounded in the ~38% human baseline: only use somatic descriptions when earned by real physical exertion, and avoid the generic AI triad of tight chests, lumps in throat, or held breath.

### 4. Punctuation, Digits & Formatting Hygiene
- STRICTLY BAN the em-dash (`—`).
- BAN colons (`:`) as narrative connectors. Do not substitute em-dashes with colons. Split thoughts into separate, clean sentences with periods (`.`).
- Use natural digits for numbers in casual digital copy ("30 detik", "2 derajat", "Rp150 rb") instead of spelling them out like print literature ("tiga puluh detik").
- BAN decorative bolding in mid-sentence (`**frasa penting**`).
- BAN rigid, symmetrical 3-point bullet lists.

### 5. Cadence, Lexicon & Anti-Savior Transition
- BAN the formulaic "Bukan sekadar X, melainkan Y" cadence and appended negation tails (*", bukan [X]"*). State positive assertions directly.
- BAN characteristic LLM buzzwords (*delve, tapestry, testament, beacon, pivotal, crucial, furthermore, elevate* / *menyelami, lentera, esensial, krusial, wadah, tonggak*).
- BAN outdated agency slang ("zonk") and archaic idioms ("tebak-tebak buah manggis").
- BAN the Savior Complex: When introducing a brand or product, do not present it as a world-saving hero. Use modest, realistic transitions ("Cherry Edu kami bikin buat bantu di bagian itu").

### 6. Dry, Actionable Closures (No Moralizing & No Bot Residue)
- Cut any concluding sermons, preachy life lessons, or motivational clichés ("Karena sukses butuh proses...").
- BAN chatbot conversational residue ("Semoga bermanfaat!", "Simak ulasannya berikut ini").
- Close with a straightforward, natural action step or leave the thought standing cleanly.

## Scope limits, state these when they apply

The study measured short stories averaging 4,753 words. The findings are strongest there.

- **Feature films and novels.** Handled via **Mode A**.
- **Short scripts and short films.** Handled via **Mode A**, with care. A five-page script has no room for a subplot, so decision 2 mostly resolves to "none" and that is fine, not an AI tell.
- **Reel scripts, captions, LinkedIn posts, ad copy.** Handled natively via **Mode C**. Do not force the 10-decision narrative sheet on short copy; execute the 6-pillar micro-copy engine instead.
- **Educational, curriculum, and explainer lessons (e.g., Cherry Edu, history & technical guides).** Explaining concepts is the primary goal, so Decision 5 (moral meaning) does not apply in the narrative sense, but follow these strict pedagogical constraints:
  - **No Didactic Career Preaching:** Never end lessons with formulaic practitioner tips or unsolicited career advice ("Bagi seorang barista: 1... 2..."). Anchor the takeaway directly to tangible, technical, or physical reality today (e.g., plant genetics, cupping table flavor profiles, or machine calibration).
  - **Documentary Narrative Flow:** Write history and theory like an engaging documentary or historical non-fiction book, not an exam review sheet or slide presentation. Avoid mid-story bullet lists and narrative colons.
  - **Domain Jargon vs AI Buzzwords:**
    - BAN synthetic LLM filler metaphors (*menyelami, lentera, wadah, tonggak, esensial, krusial, denyut nadi*).
    - PROTECT authentic industry & historical terminology (*"emas hitam"* in colonial trade history, *cupping*, *wet hulling*, *pulping*). Real practitioner vocabulary must never be over-filtered.
